## Hi there 👋

I'm a French cybersecurity engineer with a background in **Linux, networks, infrastructure and IT/OT security**. I've also worked in private investigation and OSINT, where I learned how much you can understand by connecting details that initially seem unrelated.

My current focus is **Offensive Security — OSINT-driven Reconnaissance & Attack Path Analysis**.

I like starting with a real problem: understanding what a system does, who depends on it, what an outsider can discover, and how access, permissions and trust relationships fit together. The interesting part is often finding where a seemingly ordinary access path becomes the ability to do something that matters.

I still enjoy getting my hands on systems, building things, testing assumptions and working out why something behaves differently from what the documentation suggests.

## What I focus on

- **Attack Surface Management / OSINT** — asset discovery, external exposure, reconnaissance, and the limits of what public data can establish.
- **Attack Path Analysis** — trust boundaries, identity, delegated access, effective privileges, attack paths and blast radius.
- **OT/ICS Security Assessment** — administration of industrial and physical systems, operational constraints, safeguards and recovery.

Security architecture, Linux engineering, AppSec and AI security remain part of my work. They help me understand the systems behind the findings.

## Independent investigation

### Danfoss / XWEB — Commercial Refrigeration Security Case Study

*Private technical review · October 2026*

An independent, OSINT-driven investigation into the refrigeration supervision incidents reported in French food retail.

The study brings together passive exposure research, vendor documentation, voluntary questionnaires from refrigeration professionals and competing attack-path hypotheses. It examines who may hold write privileges over refrigeration settings, how remote maintenance access is delegated, which safeguards may remain independent, and what reliable recovery requires.

The original intrusion path and the first accepted anomalous command remain unresolved. Findings are classified by evidence type, and sensitive information about third-party installations is excluded from the publication candidate.

The case study is currently undergoing private review. A public link will be added if and when it is released.

## How I work

**Start with the problem.** Understand the environment, the business constraints and the people who rely on the system.

**Connect the evidence.** Follow small clues, map relationships and question assumptions. An exposed service, a documented vulnerability and a demonstrated attack path are three different things.

**Make it useful.** Explain what is known, what remains uncertain, and which checks would help someone make a better decision.
